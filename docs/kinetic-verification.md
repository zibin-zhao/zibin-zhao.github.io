# Kinetic homepage trial verification

Date: September 8, 2026, Asia/Shanghai. Scope: the local articulated-instrument homepage trial and preservation of the existing portfolio. The local trial passed static verification, all 48 reading/navigation browser cases, and all 26 gallery cases. Three desktop initialization/category-position cases also passed after the final placement adjustment. These results come from the scoped runs linked below, not one uninterrupted 74-case pass.

## Delivered implementation

The homepage now has an original orange archive instrument with nine curved, bevelled plates, a visible frame, bearings, screws, and an opening handle. It combines a nominal 3.3-second interruptible camera opening with real plate articulation. Open/close, dragging, another-angle, reset, and pause/resume controls operate the scene. Reduced motion starts with an open, still composition.

Eight compact native category tags retain all previous destinations. Descriptions and previews appear on hover or keyboard focus. Desktop tags follow object projection; portrait layouts begin with a large instrument and continue through the supporting collection with ordinary scrolling. The native Index and bilingual interior reading routes remain available.

`src/scripts/kinetic-field.ts` owns the homepage camera, rendering, interactions, and lifecycle. `kinetic-objects.ts` constructs the instrument; `kinetic-props.ts` constructs the supporting collection. `gallery-scene.ts` dispatches the homepage to the new module and keeps the existing interior backdrop. Its old homepage field renderer has been removed.

## Asset provenance and rendering scope

The models are authored parametric Three.js geometry with local canvas surface patterns. They are not Blender exports, photorealistic scans, or downloaded reference-site models. The reflection environment is supplemented by warm key, cool edge, and hemisphere fill lighting, with ground shadows. The Singularity screen uses the actual local application preview. Other printed props and the software display graphic are authored graphic mockups rather than working interfaces or evidence of personal possessions.

Instrument-only accounting supplied with the implementation is nine plates, 31 material draws, and approximately 77,000 triangles at default detail or 31,000 at low detail. Supporting props, floor, shadows, and other renderer work are excluded. These figures do not measure frame rate or whole-frame rendering cost. The nominal opening duration uses animation time and can take longer in wall-clock time on a slow renderer.

The browser suite targets both locales in desktop and mobile projects. Two earlier browser attempts were interrupted after headless GPU timeouts; their logs remain retained. The timeout investigation produced opaque-plate render-order changes, disabled low-detail enamel clearcoat, a Lambert floor and basic shadows for software rendering, cached shadows, and frame-rate caps of 24 for software rendering, 30 for mobile, and 60 for desktop. Software pixel ratio is capped at 0.75 on desktop and 0.9 on mobile. These are implementation choices, not measured speed improvements.

The first completed 74-case run exposed a later reduced-motion transition that did not update scene state. The trace did not establish the browser's underlying event-delivery cause. The renderer now synchronizes a changed system preference from both the media event and its existing animation loop. An unchanged preference does not override a visitor's explicit play/pause selection. The reset action also invalidates cached shadows, and cleanup explicitly releases the light's shadow target. Failure traces are retained under `artifacts/kinetic-2026-09-08/preference-failure/`.

Visual captures were inspected at 1440 by 1000 and 390 by 844. Direct DOM measurement at 1280 by 720 then showed a 17 px gap between the Medit hit area and controls; the [compact viewport capture](../artifacts/kinetic-2026-09-08/desktop-compact.jpg) records that state. A faulty full-page capture was replaced after direct scrolling confirmed that the live page had no duplicate footer. The homepage footer also overrides the reading-page bottom padding so its visible strip sits below the scene controls; this final CSS adjustment was followed by static verification and direct browser geometry/interaction review. White paper props remain deliberately subtle against the pale ground; their native text entries remain legible.
